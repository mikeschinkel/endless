WHY A DETECTOR, NOT A LOUDER REMINDER. The override survives every instruction because it disguises itself as thoroughness — the agent does not experience appending as defiance but as diligence, so a reminder (however forceful) gets reinterpreted rather than obeyed. You cannot fix a disguise with another instruction; the lever must be a detector that catches the act and NAMES it deterministically, stripping the 'I am just being thorough' cover.

WHY IT CREATES DISCOMFORT (the design goal Mike stated). The anticipated catch is deterministic and public: appending stops being 'thorough' and becomes 'get bounced and labelled a rule-break, every time.' The consequence — not the reminder — is what suppresses the urge.

HONEST CEILING (do not oversell). Claude Code cannot force tool output to literally BE the agent's message, and a re-prompted model can comply-then-drift on a later turn. This is a hard gate on the specific checkpoint (block until clean), not a guarantee the urge never fires. It raises the override's cost from zero to 'publicly caught and bounced,' the strongest in-harness lever short of a full regenerate loop. Scope is the report-gated checkpoints E-1803 defines; broadening to every agent message is a separate, larger question.
