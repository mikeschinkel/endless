On 2026-11-02 a gomion planning session filed three StdIO tasks in go-dt, go-cliutil and go-cfgstore, planned a fourth in gomion, and then ran `endless task claim <id> --unattended` on all four. It wanted worktrees to move Mike's uncommitted main-checkout edits onto task branches; it did not intend to work the tasks. Each claim flipped the task to underway with the actor recorded as system, created the worktree, and bound no session, because that is what the flag does.

Eleven days later Mike found E-2551 underway, could not reach it with `session goto` ("No live session"), and had no way to tell from `task show` or the status that nobody held it. `task spawn` then adopted the task silently, because its prior-claim check reads sessions.task_id, which the unattended claim never wrote. E-2540 in gomion is still in that state.

Mike's position, 2026-11-13: a claim's entire purpose is to associate a session with a task, and a task that is underway with no session is a state that should never be allowed to occur, not one to name or display. He never asked for the flag and does not know why it is needed.

The flag came from the task that split `--force` on claim. `--force` had secretly done two things, demote a settled task and claim with no session; the code's own comment says the second "was unreachable except by accident, since nothing documented that --force did it." That task named it `--unattended` for "manual work at a terminal, a plain shell, cron." The ledgers show no cron or shell ever used it; its only recorded uses are the four above, from a Claude session.
