The ledger is append-only, and the only way to correct a task's state today is to replay ordinary transitions through their gates. When a gate was skipped on the way in, that can deadlock: a landed todo task with unapproved plan items could only get back to assumed by going through revisit, submitted, ready and underway and reconciling again, and those transitions now sit in its history as if they described real events. Accounting solves the same problem with a correcting journal entry: never erase, post a new entry that says what it corrects and why. The command should record a distinct correction event carrying the before and after status, the reason and the actor; refuse to run from an agent, as approve does; skip lifecycle gates but keep data invariants (no unknown status, no duplicate or foreign IDs); and show in task history as a correction. Repairing drift between the database and the ledger is a separate concern.
