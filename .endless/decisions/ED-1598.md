An epic may have children; a child may not. When a child starts to feel like it needs parts, that is a signal it is either several tasks that should be siblings joined by blocked_by, or one task whose parts are sections of its own plan. Why: an epic reports only the rows directly beneath it, so under nesting the count at the top is never the number of things in play; epic status is derived from children, and nesting makes that derivation recursive instead of checkable on one screen; every intermediate level adds a container that is never worked yet is read and triaged on every pass through the backlog; and where depth genuinely grouped things, the fact doing the work was a dependency, which blocked_by states precisely and schedules on. Accepted cost: grouping a middle level carried moves into the epic's own plan prose plus relations between siblings. Existing rows deeper than two levels are grandfathered and cleaned up separately, never on sight.
