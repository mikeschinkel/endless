E-2097 established that an agent-facing refusal repeats its verdict at both ends, gated on agent_facing(), while the human still sees the underlying guidance byte for byte. Carrying that split to E-2095's authority banner was proposed and rejected: the two cases are not alike. A refusal has an underlying message a human is OWED — suppress it and the command fails silently. A banner is purely additive; a person reading task show already has the status line and the Replaced by link on screen and reads them as the qualifiers they are. The banner exists because an agent does not — it quotes the row as authoritative. So the audience test is not 'is this agent-facing?' but 'is a human owed the underlying message?'. Where they are not, the whole surface is gated and a human reaches it only by asking, with --agent-view, for debugging. The alternative — render it once for humans too — was the proposal on the table and was refused as unrequested output.
