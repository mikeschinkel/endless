The worktree reaper removes directories, and it ran from five branches of the hook handler including PreToolUse and PostToolUse — a delete sweep before and after every tool call in every session. Its cost scales with the number of worktrees, which is unbounded, so any change to what it checks re-prices every tool call: making the check exact took one sweep to ninety seconds and stopped the product. Reverting to the cheap check left it at 2.58 seconds, still paid twice per call. The rule is about shape rather than the current measurement — a sweep whose cost scales with an unbounded set, and whose failure mode is deleting a directory someone still needs, does not belong on a path that runs thousands of times a day. Rejected: debouncing inside the hook, since each hook invocation is a fresh process with nowhere to keep the debounce state. Accepted cost: where nothing triggers the schedule, directories accumulate until something does.
